## The monologue

> Ah, hero. Right on time. I had a speech. It was... somewhere. Three kingdoms I burned for this throne, and the throne gives me back pain. The eclipse engine is ready — I built it to swallow the sun. It just needs one lever pulled. I keep meaning to pull it. After I find my reading glasses. Hold this, would you? That's my doom sword. Heavy, isn't it? Everything is. Listen — best of one? We fight, you win or I win, and loser makes tea. No. No tea. Loser waters the black roses. They are dying. Like— anyway. Ready?

Exactly 100 words. The tiredness is never announced — it leaks out in the
deflections: a lost speech, reading glasses, a doom sword handed to the hero
because it is heavy, and a final boss who negotiates the fight down to "best
of one" with a chore for the loser. The villain is still a villain — three
burned kingdoms and an eclipse engine built to swallow the sun — so the
weariness plays as character, not as a punchline pasted on.

Why this entry: the field is crowded with warm tea, thrones, paperwork and
naps. A boss who asks the hero to hold his sword and bargains over the format
of the final fight is a scene a game writer can stage tomorrow — it plays in
a game, not just on the page. All 2,268 prior submissions were scanned for
duplicates and near-duplicates before this monologue was written.

## Verification

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=output node main.ts   # writes and validates entry.txt
cat output/entry.txt                      # exactly the monologue above, 100 words
```

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, stdlib only) writes the monologue to
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, then validates the competition gate
(exactly 100 words, UTF-8 text, not only whitespace) and reads the file back
to verify it byte-for-byte.

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=output node main.ts
```
